Recoup

An agentic Order-to-Cash recovery system — and a working answer to the question how do you let a language model near money?

Code computes every dollar.
Every decision cites its evidence.
A human approves every external action.

Where deductions and credit decisions actually sit

Order-to-Cash is the only process whose output is cash. Recoup intervenes at two points in it.

The two ringed stages are where Recoup intervenes. Everything else is throughput.

Upstream — credit

Recoup re-underwrites from payment drift and dispute behaviour, and proposes graduated containment instead of a blunt order hold. Reads and drafts.

Midstream — deduction

Recoup reconstructs what should have been paid from delivery, contract and promo evidence, then classifies and drafts recovery. Reads, computes and drafts.

What it never does

No write-back to the ERP. No autonomous correspondence, hold or credit. Every external action stops at a human. Approves nothing on its own.

O2C failures are not all the same shape. Some delay cash, some consume working capital, and some permanently erode margin. Deductions are mostly the third kind, which is why they are worth this much machinery.

Beat 1 the seven stages. Beat 2 the two intervention points and, importantly, the third card — what Recoup does not do. Saying that early buys you credibility for everything after it.

Beat 3 the stakes framing. Deliberately not "every leak is permanent" — that is easy for a finance person to challenge, and losing that argument in the first five minutes is expensive. The three-way split is defensible.

Why this is hard in a CPG operating model

Two problems that look separate, share a root cause, and are usually solved separately.

Deduction management

  • Claims arrive through different channels and formats
  • Remittance lines often do not identify the disputed invoice or promotion
  • Proof is spread across ERP, delivery, promotions, carrier and customer systems
  • High-volume, low-value claims are uneconomic to investigate by hand
  • Duplicates and already-issued credits are hard to spot
  • Delay reduces recovery probability
  • Ownership is split across AR, sales, logistics and finance

Credit management

  • Bureau data is third-party and lagging
  • Limits rarely account for current deduction behaviour
  • Full-order holds damage revenue and the relationship
  • Exposure is spread across orders, invoices, disputes and open deductions
  • Payment-behaviour drift shows up before bureau deterioration
  • No governed way to reconcile a healthy external score with adverse first-party behaviour
  • Revised terms and partial release are negotiated manually

The root cause is the same, and it is why this is one system rather than two

Both problems are evidence problems under time pressure. And the strongest credit signal a manufacturer owns is not the bureau score — it is the customer's own deduction behaviour, which is first-party and current. A customer filing repeated invalid shortage claims is a credit event. Recoup treats it as one, then requires deterministic arbitration before anything acts on it.

Balance matters here. The earlier version of this deck was deduction-heavy and an architect noticed the credit side was thin. Give the two columns equal time.

The third beat is the one to land. If they accept that deduction behaviour is a credit signal, the Risk Mesh supervisor on slide 7 needs no further justification. If they don't, it looks like architecture for its own sake.

The same seven layers, physically

Where each layer runs, and which boundary it sits behind.

Four authorisation hops

Signed session cookie, then a signed proxy header bound to the request body, then a bearer on the API, then a separate bearer and principal on the capability boundary. Source credentials never leave the trusted compute zone.

What holds state

Rate limiting, the source cache and event fan-out are in-process. Everything durable is in the governed database. This is the constraint that blocks horizontal scaling today.

Where it fails closed

Missing governed config, missing source rows, unset run budgets or absent credentials each stop the request rather than degrade it. There are no silent defaults to fall back on.

The logical map answers "who owns what". This answers "where does it run". Architects will want both, and they will scan this one for trust boundaries first.

The point to make: source credentials sit in exactly one zone, and the capability boundary never leaves that process in the deployed topology.

Own the in-process state honestly — it is the single biggest scaling constraint and it comes back on the register at slide 15.

Expect: why two hosting platforms? Honest answer — the cockpit is a Next.js app hosted natively, and the API needs a long-lived process for streaming. Pragmatic, not designed.

Two ways a run is constrained

At design time a handoff must be declared, or constructing it throws. At run time the answer is blocked unless the trace evidences that the required handoff actually fired. Prevention and proof, at different moments.

Coverage differs today: the declared-topology check guards one edge; trace assertion guards both journeys. That gap is on the register at slide 14.

Where a run stops

Turn, step, retry and token budgets are loaded from configuration, not defaulted. If they are absent the run is blocked rather than run unbounded. A failing tool emits a typed error event instead of crashing the run.

The middle column is the point. For every agent, name the judgement it makes and the thing code takes back off it. That is the whole design in one table.

If challenged on the coverage gap: be straight. The declared-graph constructor is called on one edge today. Runtime trace assertion covers both journeys, so behaviour is constrained everywhere — but the design-time contract is incomplete, and it is on the risk register rather than hidden.

Evidence: agentRuntime.ts, handoffGraph.ts, messages.ts, conductor.ts, creditRiskQuerySession.ts.

Pattern selection — and deliberate restraint

The lowest orchestration complexity that satisfies the requirement.

PatternUsed here?Why
Single agent with toolsyesScoped query journeys, narrow capability set, pinned evidence scope
SequentialyesForensics to Recovery Drafter; credit assessment to action packet. The next stage is predefined
Supervisor, agents-as-toolsyesRisk Mesh gathers advisory positions; deterministic code arbitrates, not the supervisor
Dynamic handoff routingpartialThe SDK mechanism exists, but the next agent is prescribed and then asserted — so this is not open routing
Concurrent fan-out / fan-innoNo parallel agent execution; parallel tool calls are explicitly disabled
Group chat / agent debatenoNot implemented. Consensus by conversation is not a control we would want here
Maker-checker agentsnoValidation is a deterministic service, not a critic agent
Adaptive / task-ledger planningnoInappropriate where the output is money leaving a company

Four of these are marked no on purpose. In a domain with consequential external actions, the interesting engineering is in what you decline to make dynamic.

This slide exists to pre-empt pattern-bingo. Architects have read the Microsoft orchestration guide; some will be checking which patterns we claim. Claiming fewer, with reasons, reads as maturity.

The "partial" row matters most. An SDK handoff mechanism being present does not make routing dynamic — the tool choice is forced and the resulting handoff is then asserted. Overstating that is the easiest credibility loss on this slide.

Framework reference: Microsoft's AI agent orchestration patterns, whose useful principle is lowest sufficient complexity rather than pattern coverage.

Every capability is classified before it can be called.

The registry is broader than what any agent can see. Six capabilities are reachable by a model; the financial and decision tools are not among them.

Three enforcement points, not one

The capability list is filtered before the model sees it; the server re-checks visibility on every call; and the evidence scope travels on the request rather than being asked for by the model. A forged call fails at the boundary.

MCP is not the integration layer

This is the distinction that most often gets blurred. MCP governs what an agent may ask for. Adapters govern how a source is reached. There is no general-purpose ERP query door — an agent gets scoped questions, never a connection.

agent → scoped capability → service tool → canonical evidence request → adapter → source

The consequence classes are the reusable idea. Classify by blast radius, then derive exposure and authorisation from the classification. Adding a capability without a class becomes a type error, so you cannot forget to secure one.

Land the second panel deliberately. If they leave thinking MCP is the enterprise integration layer, slide 10 will not make sense.

Evidence: serviceLayer.ts registry and metadata, permissionEngine.ts, mcp/server.ts, mcpGateway.ts.

L5 — the deterministic decision spine live

Everything that becomes a number is computed here, and only here.

Money is a type, not a primitive

Fixed-precision decimal throughout. The money schema accepts a decimal or a decimal string and rejects a floating-point number outright, so a float cannot enter the money path even by accident.

One rule, one module

Eight named rules, each owning a single deduction pattern. Every finding names the rule that produced it, so a verdict is never an opinion with a number attached.

Idempotent by construction

An event identity is derived from the rule, the sorted record IDs and the period. Re-running a sweep over the same data cannot double-count a recovery.

The core recomputes what the model proposes

A proposed hold split is recalculated and rejected if it disagrees. A recovery amount is clamped to the computed variance. The model may suggest; the core decides.

The claim this supports — stated precisely

Deterministic controls prevent the model from asserting a consequential amount or dispatching an action. They do not make agent judgement harmless: a poor tool choice or an incomplete evidence request still costs coverage and latency. What they bound is the class of failure, not its existence.

This is the answer to the sceptic, and the last panel is the honest version of it. An earlier draft of this deck claimed a wrong agent judgement could only produce a missed finding. That was too strong and a reviewer caught it — a wrong tool choice or a partial evidence request affects coverage and latency too. Bounding the failure class is the defensible claim; eliminating failure is not.

The float-rejection detail usually gets a nod. It is small, and it shows the discipline is enforced rather than aspired to.

Evidence: types/money.ts, core/rules/, types/variance.ts, core/partialHold.ts, guardrails/tool/amountClamp.ts.

Readiness is a state, not a hope

Each connector reports ready, ready_synthetic, blocked_credentials_required or blocked_schema_required.

Across L2 and L5 — what remembers what live

Six kinds of state, and only one of them is the truth.

MechanismHoldsAuthoritative?

Case and transaction memoryDurable operational stateno
Read modelsBusiness-facing current stateno
Vector retrievalA search indexno
Cited source records and audit chainDecision history and basisyes

Memory makes a workflow continuous. It never becomes the source of financial truth, and it cannot substitute for a cited record.

Memory is typed and refuses what it should not hold

Eleven categories, three trust levels, and a schema that rejects personal data and secrets at validation time — so a record containing them never becomes a record. Compaction is deterministic and inherits the worst trust level of its inputs, so it cannot launder an untrusted note into a trusted summary.

Model choice is a deployment decision, not a runtime one

Models bind per agent at construction and an agent cannot change its own model. Routing today is by reasoning effort, not by model tier — and one declared tier is unused. Moving the low-effort agents to a smaller model is the obvious next cost lever and has not been pulled.

Caching is structural, not incidental

The prompt is assembled as a stable governance and capability prefix followed by case evidence. Only the prefix is cached, under a versioned per-capability key. Case evidence sits after the cache boundary, so two cases share governance text and nothing else.

The table is the point. Architects have seen systems where a memory store quietly becomes the system of record. Being explicit that only cited records and the audit chain are authoritative is a differentiator.

Prefix-only caching is the answer to how do you know evidence is not leaking between cases — the prefix contains no case data by construction.

Be candid about the unused model tier. It reads as having read your own code.

Evidence: memory/schema.ts, memory/compaction.ts, config/models.ts, agents/promptAssembly.ts, config/openaiPromptCache.ts.

Cross-cutting — consequence, guardrails and the human gate live

Controls are placed by consequence, not by convenience.

ConsequenceExampleControl
Read-only retrievalRead invoice or delivery evidenceScoped capability and adapter
Deterministic computeVariance or hold splitDecimal-safe core function
Local draftRecovery or proposed termsStructured schema plus evidence basis
Decision recordVerdict or containment proposalDeterministic validation, then audit
External actionCorrespondence, hold, ERP writeHuman approval; no ERP write path exists

Human approval here is an architectural boundary, not a confirmation dialog. The approver must be a human principal and can never be the proposer.

Guardrails sit at three boundaries

Before anything reaches the model, around every decision-producing tool, and at the final output. Tool-level guards exist because agent-level guards do not run at every handoff or agents-as-tools boundary — and those gaps are exactly where a multi-agent system leaks.

The model's prose never reaches the reader

Generated text is suppressed in the live path; what the analyst reads is assembled by code from cited records. The model drives the investigation, it does not narrate the finding. This is deliberately aggressive and worth debating.

No write path, as a structural property

Not disabled — absent. A static check asserts no write-capable ERP client is ever constructed, and a module-graph rule stops one being introduced.

Lead with the consequence table. It is the reusable idea: classify by blast radius, then let the control follow from the class rather than from someone remembering.

The prose-suppression panel usually provokes a reaction. Invite the debate — in this domain everything true is already in the cited record, so narrative adds risk without adding information. In a domain where the prose is the product you would choose differently.

Evidence: guardrails/ (input, tool, output), services/approvals.ts, invariants covering HITL, segregation of duties and the absent write path.

Cross-cutting — how it is checked, and how it is watched

Four evaluation layers, four observability layers, and one honest hole.

Evaluation not CI-enforced

  • Invariant tests — money, evidence, authorisation and the no-write contract
  • Gold-set evaluation — labelled parity, false-positive and accuracy gates
  • Agent-path evaluation — required tool calls, handoffs, receipts, budgets, guardrail trips
  • Production monitoring — source health, latency, tokens, cache, approval behaviour

Offline gates are code-graded, not model-judged. The gap: these run on demand, not on every push — so treat them as a discipline, not yet as an automated release control.

Observability partial

  • Business — case status, verdict, approval, cited records
  • Agent — turns, tool calls, handoffs, retries, guardrail trips
  • Model and cost — tokens including cached, latency, model, cache key
  • Platform — source health, dependency errors, correlation IDs

All four carry the same correlation identifier, so one request can be followed from business outcome to token spend.

Auditability is the strong half

An append-only, hash-chained record with compare-and-swap on the tail. For why did this decision happen, and on what basis, the answer is complete and tamper-evident.

Runtime observability is the weak half

No distributed tracing, no structured logging, no trace backend. The join key exists; the query is manual. For why is this slow right now there is no good answer yet. Instrumentation is target state, not current.

Draw the distinction explicitly: auditability and observability are different properties, and this system is strong on the first and weak on the second. Teams routinely conflate them and assume a good audit trail implies good operations.

The correlation identifier is the useful detail — it means adopting distributed tracing later is wiring rather than re-architecture, because the join key is already threaded through.

Do not present tracing as current. It is not.

What transfers — then the live system

Four patterns worth stealing, whatever you are automating.

Split by consequence, not capability

Let the model choose what to look at; let code produce anything a human will act on. Then make the split a test so it cannot erode under delivery pressure.

Capabilities are a classified registry

Classify by risk and side effect, derive exposure and authorisation from the class. Adding one without a class should fail the build.

Audit the trace, do not trust it

Assert that specific receipts exist before releasing an answer. A trace nobody checks is decoration.

Put a contract at the source boundary

Canonical evidence with provenance and a readiness state is what stops a customer's different source system becoming a re-platforming project.
